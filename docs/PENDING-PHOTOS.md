Last updated: 1 October 2026.

The site finds a product's picture purely by filename — `<slug>.jpg` in
`public/images/products/`. A product with no file shows the striped
"Photograph required" placeholder, which is deliberate and visible, so a gap
is never mistaken for a working page.

The authoritative list is the `AWAITING` array in `test/enquiry.test.ts`.
Supplying a photograph means dropping the file in and deleting a line there.
That is on purpose: nobody can quietly point a product at a file that does not
exist and have it look the same as a product we are legitimately waiting on.

## Held back, not published

**The Garware rope poster.** It is another manufacturer's registered
trademark, logo and artwork, used as the whole design. Putting it on this site
states a relationship with that company. Do not publish it unless the client
confirms in writing that he is an authorised dealer and may use their brand
assets — and even then, a photograph of the stock he actually holds is the
safer picture.

**The running-track image.** Not a netting product, and the lettering rendered
in the image is malformed. If the client wants it, it belongs on the Services
page as a project, with a real location attached, not in the catalogue.

## One thing the client should be told

This batch is computer-generated marketing artwork, not photographs of his own
installations — the scenes, the people and the sites in them are synthetic, and
the logo in several of them is not the mark used everywhere else on the site.

That is his call to make, not ours. But it is worth him knowing that the rest
of the catalogue is real work on real sites, and that a customer who notices
the difference will wonder which of the two he is looking at. Photographs of
his own stock and his own completed jobs are worth more here than any poster.
